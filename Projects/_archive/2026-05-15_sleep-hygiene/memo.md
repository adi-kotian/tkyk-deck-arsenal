# Memo — The Importance of Sleep Hygiene

## One-line pitch
Sleep is the highest-leverage performance investment a founder can make, and unlike most edges, it compounds.

## Core thesis
Founders optimize for output and treat sleep as a discretionary cost. That math is backwards: a single short night degrades reaction time, working memory, and risk calibration to a degree most operators would never accept from a key hire. Protecting sleep is not self-care — it is the cheapest, most reliable performance enhancer available, and the returns compound across decisions, code, and relationships over a company's life.

## Key points (3–5)

1. **Sleep loss is a tax on judgment, not just energy.** Decades of sleep research consistently show that mild chronic restriction (6 hours a night) produces measurable deficits in attention, working memory, and risk assessment — the exact faculties a founder bills hourly. The deficit is invisible from the inside; self-rated alertness recovers long before performance does.

2. **The "I'll sleep when it ships" trade is mispriced.** An extra waking hour at 70% capacity is worth less than the rested hour it costs you tomorrow, and the gap widens with every consecutive short night. Founders who stack short nights are paying compound interest on a loan they don't see on the books.

3. **Sleep is a force multiplier on every other input.** Caffeine, exercise, diet, and even therapy all work better on a rested brain. Sleep is the substrate; the rest are amplifiers. Skip the substrate and you're tuning a guitar with no strings.

4. **Hygiene beats heroics.** You cannot out-supplement, out-meditate, or out-Oura-ring a chaotic schedule. The leverage lives in three boring inputs — consistent wake time, light exposure in the first hour, and a hard caffeine cutoff — that cost nothing and require no new identity.

5. **One habit, started this week, beats a perfect protocol started never.** The audience does not need a 14-step routine; they need the single change with the highest expected value for their current bottleneck. Pick one and ship it tonight.

## Tone
Dry, operator-to-operator, slightly contrarian. Closer to a Patrick Collison essay or a Stripe internal memo than to a Headspace blog post. No "wellness journey" language, no soft-focus stock photos, no exclamation points. Treat the audience as smart adults who already know they should sleep more and have rationalized their way out of it.

## Out of scope
- Sleep disorders (apnea, insomnia, RLS). Those are medical, not behavioral, and need a clinician.
- Detailed neuroscience or stage-by-stage sleep architecture. Interesting, but the audience needs leverage, not lectures.
- Product recommendations (mattresses, trackers, supplements). Hygiene is behavioral; gear is a distraction.
- Lifestyle redesign — work-life-balance framing. The deck wins by staying inside the founder's existing value system, not by trying to replace it.
